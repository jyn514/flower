[My last blog post](/an-engineers-perspective-on-hiring/) got a lot of attention. I got 6 emails, a request to repost the link to LinkedIn ([done](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7361088829987938305/)) and over 600 comments on Hacker News, Lobsters, and Reddit [^1]. Some of those emails were really heart-felt. Thank you.

I haven't read all 600 comments, let alone replied, but i read a fair number of them. Some suggested alternate interview processes i hadn't seen before (see [below](#alternate-interview-approaches)). There was disagreement on was the right hiring approach was, but pretty strong agreement that the existing ones are broken. And there was significant disagreement on whether "most interview candidates are good, and companies should focus on whether they're a good 'fit' for the team" or whether "most interview candidates are bad, and companies can't lower their standards or they'll hire people who can't program".

Let's look at these in reverse order.
## [scarcity, abundance, and lost hires](https://ansuz.sooke.bc.ca/entry/372)
One of my favorite posts about hiring is titled "[Scarcity, Abundance, and Lost Careers](https://ansuz.sooke.bc.ca/entry/372)" by Matthew Skala. He posits that there are two situations an organization can *be* in for hiring, scarcity and abundance, and the same two situations an organization can *think* they are in, but they can be wrong. He calls factual abundance and imagined scarcity "inflation" and factual scarcity and imagined abundance "dissipation".
